    Key takeaways from the Ansible course¶

    Andrea Dainese ansible beginner

    We have seen that Ansible is an excellent tool for improving operations by automating tasks and orchestrating processes. Its procedural structure makes it easy to transform manual tasks into automated ones: if we can describe a task, we can build a playbook to implement it.

    When properly structured, a playbook not only becomes the official way of operating, but also documents the process itself.

    However, Ansible is not the only framework available, and it is important to understand its strengths and limitations.

    Ansible is well suited to those without a programming background, which is why it is often my first choice when I approach automation projects.

    Organizations rarely have the capacity to develop and maintain new modules, so in that sense the scope of Ansible is limited to the existing modules.

    From a performance perspective, there are more efficient frameworks than Ansible. Initially this is rarely a concern, but as projects grow, different needs may emerge.
