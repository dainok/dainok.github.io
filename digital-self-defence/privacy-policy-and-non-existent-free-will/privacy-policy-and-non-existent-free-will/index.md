**Disclaimer:** this article replaces the one I originally planned, because I noticed something important that caught my attention. Despite the title, this is not about privacy. If you landed here attracted by the title, at least read the conclusions at the end.

As you probably know, WhatsApp recently updated its privacy policy. The news triggered a massive exodus of users towards alternative messaging apps. **Bad idea, really bad.**

## Privacy Policy

A privacy policy is supposed to explain how a service processes personal data, especially in Europe, where the GDPR applies. I say "supposed to" because the reality is often more complex.

To me, the privacy policy is a **litmus test** of how much a company cares about data protection. Medium and large companies that offer multiple services *should* update their policies often: laws change, technologies evolve, partnerships shift and services (hopefully) become safer and more user-friendly over time.

WhatsApp has updated its privacy policy [several times](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal?doc=privacy-policy&mode=revisions): in 2016, 2019, 2020 and again in 2021. For European users, nothing dramatically new has changed. To dive deeper into the legal side, I suggest [Altroconsumo](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/sicurezza/privacy/avviso-privacy-whatsapp-ecco-cosa-non-torna-i-nodi-giuridici/), [Cybersecurity360](https://www.cybersecurity360.it/news/nuova-privacy-policy-whatsapp-tanti-timori-sulla-condivisione-dati-ecco-perche/) and [Agenda Digitale](https://www.altroconsumo.it/hi-tech/smartphone/news/whatsapp-aggiornamento-privacy).

One crucial point: WhatsApp falls under U.S. surveillance laws and therefore **must grant access to personal data, even of non-U.S. citizens, regardless of where that data is stored**. This has always been true. Nothing has changed.

Finally, in my experience, privacy policies never fully reflect reality. Processes are often undocumented, details are omitted and mistakes are left in. The bigger the company, the bigger the gap. That is why I call them litmus tests: **if a company cares about data, its processes and technologies evolve, and so should its privacy policies.**

## Manipulation of People

Here is the real issue.

As I said, little has changed. WhatsApp was subject to U.S. surveillance law before, and it still is. If that was a problem today, it was already a problem last month.

And yet a huge number of people uninstalled WhatsApp and moved to Signal or Telegram, not based on facts, but on an **emotional wave of fear**, amplified by media noise and viral social posts. This is **THE PROBLEM**.

I personally received messages full of false claims, with no references or sources, crafted solely to spread fear. Classic **fake news**. Fake news has a dual purpose: it manipulates emotions, and it censors dialogue (by shutting down critical thinking).

This fear-driven messaging convinced millions of people to take action without asking themselves whether it made sense. **That is manipulation, not choice.**

As [Pavel Durov](https://t.me/s/durov) shared, Telegram gained 25 million users in just 72 hours. 27% were Europeans: 6.7 million people switching platforms in three days, not out of rational analysis, but because of emotional contagion.

## Free Will

A wise teacher once said that, given an event, free will lies in choosing the emotion with which we respond to it. The obvious consequence is that if I can manipulate your emotions around an event, you *believe* you are choosing freely, while in reality you act exactly as I designed.

If we binge-watch a fearmongering video and let emotions drive our decisions, **free will disappears**.

## Threat Modeling

This is the real threat we should worry about: **the manipulation of our perception.**

Before rushing into a new messaging app, we need to ask ourselves: *who am I entrusting my information to, and what kind of data are they really collecting?*

If you have never done such an exercise, try it now:

* List the threats of using WhatsApp.
* Rank them by severity.
* Then decide whether switching makes sense, and to what.

Only then should you look at resources like this [messaging comparison table](https://inteltechniques.com/messaging.html).

## Privacy Protection

If your concern is broader, the constant surveillance by almost any online service, brace yourself. It is a long, steep road. And no, deleting a single app will not solve it. We will have the chance to discuss that another time.

## Conclusions

For European users, nothing substantial has changed with the WhatsApp privacy policy. If privacy is the issue, it was already there. If trust is the issue, it was already broken.

But that is not the point.

I deliberately used current news, a sensitive topic and a catchy title to grab your attention, just like the manipulation I described. The difference is that my goal was to make you aware of the mechanism.

Two kinds of readers may have landed here:

* Regular followers (thank you): I hope you still found a fresh perspective.
* Worried WhatsApp users: I hope this helped you see how easily fear can drive actions, and why structured thinking, like threat modeling, is the real defense.

Greater awareness and critical thinking do not just make individuals safer: they also strengthen companies and digital ecosystems.
