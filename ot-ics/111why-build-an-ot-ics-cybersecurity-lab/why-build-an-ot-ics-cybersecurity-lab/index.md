title: Why build an OT/ICS Cybersecurity lab?
---
For several years now, I have been deeply interested in the security challenges of Industrial Control Systems (ICS) and Operational Technology (OT). I live in a region renowned for its manufacturing excellence, and many of my clients operate in this domain. This proximity has let me witness firsthand how ICS/OT security is evolving.

When I first started talking about applying cybersecurity to ICS/OT systems, I quickly realized I was ahead of the curve. At the time, the primary focus in ICS/OT environments was operational continuity. OT managers cared more about uptime, process efficiency and reliability than about the risks posed by cyber threats.

The landscape has been shifting gradually. Traditionally isolated ICS/OT systems have started to interact more closely with IT environments. The trend accelerated significantly around 2020, when connecting ICS/OT systems to the Internet for real-time remote support and monitoring became common practice.

This convergence of IT and OT brings both opportunities and challenges. On the one hand, connectivity improves efficiency and reduces the response time of troubleshooting. On the other hand, it exposes previously isolated systems to cyber threats they were not designed to handle.

To deepen my understanding, experiment with ICS/OT-specific security solutions and give meaningful demonstrations to clients and stakeholders, I needed dedicated environments, part physical and part virtual. These labs are controlled spaces where I can test vulnerabilities, evaluate defensive mechanisms and simulate real-world attack scenarios.

In this series of posts, I explore the different approaches to building ICS/OT labs: from hardware configurations to virtualized environments, and from affordable setups to more advanced scenarios. My aim is to provide a practical guide for anyone interested in this field.
